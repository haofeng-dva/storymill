The funeral home smelled of overwatered lilies and the particular chemical sweetness of preservative sprays. Mara stood at the back of the room, her black pumps sinking into the thick carpet with each small shift of weight. The floral displays ringed the casket in a tight horseshoe—white roses, gladioli, one elaborate spray shaped like a crucifix. She'd sent none of them. She'd been too busy with the death certificate, the probate paperwork, the call to the life insurance company.

The invoice lay on the podium beside the guest book, beneath a paperweight shaped like a dove. Someone had set it down carelessly, face-up. Mara's eyes caught the header: Eternal Petals, Florist. The line item read Large Resurrection Spray, hand-arranged, premium taper candles (set of 6). Total: $1,280. The billing address was her own home.

She picked it up. The paper was warm from the lamp on the podium. The date was two days before Mark's heart attack. The order had been placed online, the credit card number ending in 4407. Mark's card had ended in 2191. She knew because she had reconciled his statements every month for eleven years.

She turned the invoice over. No note. No delivery instructions. Just the order, the price, and the name of the deceased: Mark A. Carver. But the buyer field was blank.

Mara looked at the casket. The lilies were too white, the spray too large. The premium tapers—she counted six—were unlit, their wicks clean. Not a single one had been burned.
